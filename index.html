Field Dispatch #01 · Madison & Belvedere · Summer 1985

Dancing Jimmy: Stag, Streetlights, and Midtown After Dark

"Living above my mom’s antique store at Madison & Belvedere, I got hired to mow the corner park across the street for $35—the exact price of a Libertyland season pass. First week on the job, I learned what I really signed up for: extracting empty Stag bottles from the azaleas, and once a week, pulling Dancing Jimmy out of the shrubs and hosing the vomit and sweat off him. Midtown life was rich..."

Field Dispatch #02 · 1783 Union Rear · 1991–1993

Babylon Café: Hand-Rolled Pasta, the Herb Room, and Union Ave After Dark

"Tucked in the gravel alleyway behind 1783 Union was a two-story wooden clapboard sanctuary that smelled like fresh rosemary, hand-cranked pasta, and dark roasted coffee. Between 1991 and 1993, I was the café’s all-around 'Boy Friday.' It was the kind of room where Cyndi Lauper could slip in the back door after soundcheck, Laurie Anderson could sit quietly over an espresso, or a delegation of Tibetan monks in saffron robes could dine at midnight without a single soul staring..."

Field Dispatch #03 · 1588 Madison Ave · 1981–1995

The Antenna Club: Tin Ceilings, Sticky Floors, and 1588 Madison After Dark

"If you walked into 1588 Madison on a Tuesday night in the eighties or nineties, your sneakers instantly stuck to the linoleum. Narrow as a bowling alley, smelling of clove cigarettes, damp leather, and decades of spilled draft beer. Black Flag, Sonic Youth, The Replacements, Hüsker Dü, and Alex Chilton tore through that tiny stage. You were close enough to get sweat flung onto your shirt when the drummer hit the crash cymbal. It was where the city kept its teeth..."

Field Dispatch #04 · Madison Avenue · Midtown Bohemian Era

Decadence Manor: Porch Lounging, Architectural Salvage, and Madison Ave After Dark

"If you ever spent a summer night walking the sidewalk of Madison Avenue in the golden age of Midtown, you knew the porch before you even reached the gate. A sprawling wooden manor framed by blooming Southern magnolias, thorny heirloom roses, grandfather clock faces, and an uncurated garden of architectural salvage. It was a living, breathing sanctuary for artists, night owls, and lovable eccentrics who refused to live in beige suburbs..."
