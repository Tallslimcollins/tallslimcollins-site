Babylon Café: Hand-Rolled Pasta, the Herb Room, and Union Ave After Dark

Field Dispatch #03 · 1588 Madison Ave · 1981–1995

The Antenna Club: Tin Ceilings, Sticky Floors, and 1588 Madison After Dark

"If you walked into 1588 Madison on a Tuesday night in the eighties or nineties, your sneakers instantly stuck to the linoleum. Narrow as a bowling alley, smelling of clove cigarettes, damp leather, and decades of spilled draft beer. Black Flag, Sonic Youth, The Replacements, Hüsker Dü, and Alex Chilton tore through that tiny stage. You were close enough to get sweat flung onto your shirt when the drummer hit the crash cymbal. It was where the city kept its teeth..."

[CONTENT WORLD 05 // FIELD NOTES]

The Notebook: Observations on Craft, Scale & Honest Things

Unfiltered notes from four decades of workrooms, night hours, and salvage heaps.

NOTE #01 // SCALE & HONESTY

The Architecture of Weight

"If a timber frame isn't plumb, you feel it in your bones. If fabric isn't weighted properly, it falls lifeless."

Scale isn't about making things big—it's about whether an object has enough physical presence to hold a room without shouting. Most modern design overlooks weight. When something is built honest, you don't have to explain it.

NOTE #02 // PATINA VS. DISTRESS

You Cannot Fake Time

"Factory distressing is a corporate lie. Real patina is thirty years of morning sunlight and coat sleeves."

Real patina is thirty years of sunlight hitting an armoire door, coat sleeves rubbing against the arm of a Directoire chair, and copper oxidizing in Southern humidity. You can't manufacture character in an afternoon.

NOTE #03 // HOMES THAT WORK

Revive, Don't Replace

"You don't need a new house. You need better light, fewer distractions, and meaningful things."

People usually don't call because they need a picture hung. They call because life changed. Someone moved out. Someone passed away. They bought an old house and got overwhelmed. My job isn't just fixing a room—it's helping people fall back in love with where they live.
