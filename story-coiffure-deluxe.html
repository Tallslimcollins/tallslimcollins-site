Field Dispatch #05 · Cooper-Young & Super 8 Film · 1994

Coiffure Deluxe: Blackbird Blue, Heated Steel & Will Murray’s ’76 Monte Carlo

Robin Tucker, Hollywoods Design at 966 S. Cooper St., Sallie Sabbatini’s 35mm lens, and cutting up on the set of the 1994 Super 8 film Blown.

Era: Summer 1994 Location: Midtown & Cooper-Young, Memphis Photograph: Sallie Sabbatini Vehicle: 1976 Chevrolet Monte Carlo
Stevan Collins Lazich (Coiffure Deluxe) & Bryan Cournoyer in Will Murray’s 1976 Monte Carlo on the set of the 1994 Super 8 film Blown. Photograph by Sallie Sabbatini.

I met Robin in 1994—the exact same year we opened Otherlands Coffee Bar inside Cottland Bedding Company right down the street in Cooper-Young. I had a head full of hair that hung past my shoulders, so the natural thing to do was have Robin Tucker cut it and put the deepest darkest deposit of Wella No1 Black hair dye on it.

I wanted the neck of a blackbird, the way it shines, so she put a blue wash over that black.

“I wanted the neck of a blackbird, the way it shines, so she put a blue wash over that black.”

The concoction of pomade was one Joel Hilgenberg and I mixed: Memphis-made Black and White pomade (only available in England at the time), Murray’s orange can pomade, and 3 dashes of liquid Tres Flores hair dressing oil.

You couldn’t get a brush or garden rake through that so I had a Stainless Steel 10” comb that had to be heated to comb through the wax. (Yes, it was sharpened so it doubled as a razor knife).

This is what we referred to as my COIFFURE DELUXE and can be seen captured in this ridiculous photo Sallie Sabbatini took of me on set with Bryan Cournoyer in Will Murray’s ’76 Monte Carlo. We were cuttin' up in the 90s!!

Cooper-Young Ground Zero

Hollywoods Design & Maxwell’s

Robin Tucker’s salon and barbershop stood at 966 S. Cooper St. Decades earlier, Priscilla Presley had her hair styled in this room; years later, Karen Carrier transformed the space into the beloved Beauty Shop Restaurant.

Directly across the corner was Maxwell’s—where Stevan met his brother Shea and learned bartending sitting across the wood from Memphis’s two finest ever: David Parks (“The Sheriff of the Twilight Zone”) and Sallie Annaser (“The Monster of Martinis”), both of whom Stevan credited when named one of Memphis’s Best Bartenders by Thrillist.
