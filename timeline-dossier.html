1980s – 1990s Drapery Workrooms, Scale & French Antiquities
FILE ENTRY #01 // MADISON & BELVEDERE

The Workroom Apprentice & The Architecture of Scale

Living above my mother’s antique store at the corner of Madison & Belvedere in the heart of Midtown Memphis, education didn't happen in air-conditioned lecture halls. It happened on workroom cutting tables, surrounded by heavy bolts of French silk damask, cast brass fittings, and aged walnut patina.

Working in European drapery and antique restoration taught me what most modern design overlooks: weight, structural proportion, and honesty. If a timber frame wasn't plumb, you felt it. If fabric wasn't weighted correctly, it fell lifeless. That tactile standard permanently anchored my visual vocabulary.

Between shifts, Midtown streetlife was raw and unscripted. Mowing the corner park across from the shop for $35 to earn a Libertyland season pass meant pulling empty Stag bottles from azalea shrubs and hosing down neighborhood legends like Dancing Jimmy under the streetlights.

1990s – 2010s Subculture, DJ Decks & Midtown Nightlife
FILE ENTRY #02 // UNION REAR & 1588 MADISON

Sanctuaries of Lost Memphis: Babylon, Antenna & Midnight Vinyl

Between 1991 and 1993, I operated as the all-around "Boy Friday" at Babylon Café, tucked into the gravel alleyway behind 1783 Union Rear. Hand-rolling pasta on long wooden boards, simmering scratch marinara, and grinding espresso in the Herb Room while Cyndi Lauper, Laurie Anderson, or delegations of Tibetan monks in saffron robes slipped in through the back door at midnight.

Down the street at 1588 Madison Avenue, The Antenna Club was where underground Memphis kept its teeth. Narrow as a bowling alley, with tin ceiling tiles rattling from bass amps and sneakers sticking to the linoleum floor. Black Flag, Sonic Youth, The Replacements, and Alex Chilton tore through that stage without velvet ropes or corporate sponsor banners.

Running Serato turntables and DJ decks through late-night warehouse sets and Midtown rooms cemented an obsession with tempo, typography, and underground flyer culture that still drives every apparel artifact produced today.

2010s – 2020s Conservation, Fine Art & Studio Practice
FILE ENTRY #03 // OVERTON PARK & COOPER-YOUNG

Salvaging the Southern Gothic: Murals, Canvas & Decadence

As historic Midtown faced waves of developer demolition and beige drywall renovations, my studio practice shifted into deliberate preservation. Decadence Manor on Madison Avenue stood as a living monument: grandfather clock faces leaning against porch railings, motorcycle frames resting among magnolia blossoms, and artists lounging on velvet daybeds until sunrise.

This decade became an era of intense canvas production, custom architectural mural work, and screenprint experimentation. Capturing architectural salvage before the bulldozers arrived, and translating oral lore—the Sharecropper Sisters of the Arkansas Delta, the Cooper-Young Craftsman bungalows, the Overton Park Lanterns—into physical artifacts that couldn't be erased.

Present Day Spottswood House & The Dual Practice
FILE ENTRY #04 // SPOTTSWOOD CORRIDOR, 901

The Dual Practice: Home Revival & TallSlimCollins

Today, operating out of Spottswood House in Memphis, the work is channeled through two complementary disciplines:

Home Revival by Stevan Collins Lazich: A high-touch residential transformation service specializing in historic color palette development, architectural carpentry salvage, and museum-grade gallery wall curation. Hands-on, unpretentious, and dedicated to restoring soul to historic homes.

TallSlimCollins: A cultural archive and wearable design house. Every shirt, print, and cap is fulfilled via Threadless as a tangible Mid-South memoir—from the Shop Dog Society honoring working shop hounds to field dispatches of lost Midtown sanctuaries.
