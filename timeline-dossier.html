1980s – 1990s Drapery Workrooms, Scale & French Antiquities
FILE ENTRY #01A // 583 S. MCLEAN & 1707 MADISON

The Antique Shop Sink & The Architecture of Scale

In 1984, in the fourth grade, my family moved to Memphis to renovate 583 S. McLean. By 1985, home was literally the interior of an antique store at 1707 Madison Avenue. Education didn't happen in air-conditioned classrooms—it happened on workroom cutting tables, surrounded by heavy bolts of French silk damask, cast brass fittings, and aged walnut patina.

When you're a fifth-grader taking nightly baths in a stainless commercial kitchen sink between 18th-century French armoires, you develop an intimate, unromantic relationship with physical objects. That summer going into sixth grade, I rode home with Mom in a cargo van loaded to the roof with French antiques—spending two full days riding and sleeping directly on the solid wooden base of an authentic Louis XVI-style armoire. You learn what most modern designers never touch: weight, structural proportion, and honesty. If a timber frame wasn't plumb, you felt it in your bones. If drapery fabric wasn't weighted, it fell lifeless.

Midtown streetlife was raw and unscripted. Mowing the corner park across from the shop for $35 to earn a Libertyland season pass meant pulling empty Stag bottles from azalea shrubs and hosing down neighborhood legends like Dancing Jimmy under radiating streetlights.

FILE ENTRY #01B // COOPER-YOUNG & SUN STUDIO

Cottland Bedding, Otherlands Buildout & Sun Studio (1992–1997)

In 1992, I began at Cottland Bedding Company, learning retail craft, futons, and exotic gifts. In 1994, we stripped plaster, framed partitions, and renovated the adjoining space to open Otherlands Coffee Bar in Cooper-Young. It became an institution: precision barista standards, sensory cleanliness, and gift buying.

During those Midtown years, I worked the front door at Automatic Slim’s for Karen Carrier—checking IDs for legends like Link Wray—and spent the sweltering summer of 1997 working seasonal hours at Sun Studio, right when Parliament-Funkadelic tore through the University of Memphis. It cemented the intersection of historic preservation, live performance, and physical space.

FILE ENTRY #01C // 966 S. COOPER ST. · SUPER 8 FILM

Coiffure Deluxe, Hollywoods Design & Will Murray’s ’76 Monte Carlo (1994)

In 1994—the exact same year we opened Otherlands Coffee Bar in Cottland Bedding—I met Robin Tucker down the street at Hollywoods Design (966 S. Cooper St., the space that later became Karen Carrier’s legendary Beauty Shop Restaurant). Having hair past my shoulders, Robin cut it and deposited Wella No. 1 Black with a blue wash over it: “I wanted the neck of a blackbird, the way it shines.”

Joel Hilgenberg and I concocted the pomade: Memphis-made Black and White (only available in England then), Murray’s orange can, and three dashes of liquid Tres Flores hair dressing oil—requiring a 10” razor-sharpened stainless steel comb heated to slice through the wax. Captured on set by photographer Sallie Sabbatini with Bryan Cournoyer in Will Murray’s ’76 Monte Carlo during the filming of the 1994 Super 8 motion picture Blown.

1990s – 2010s Subculture, VIP Hosting & Midnight Vinyl
FILE ENTRY #02A // 1783 UNION REAR & 1588 MADISON

Babylon Café & The Antenna Club: Midnight In Midnight Memphis

Between 1991 and 1993, I operated as the all-around "Boy Friday" at Babylon Café, tucked into the gravel alleyway behind 1783 Union Rear. Hand-rolling pasta on long wooden boards, simmering scratch marinara, and grinding espresso in the Herb Room while Cyndi Lauper, Laurie Anderson, or delegations of Tibetan monks in saffron robes slipped in through the back door at midnight.

Down the street at 1588 Madison Avenue, The Antenna Club was where underground Memphis kept its teeth. Narrow as a bowling alley, with tin ceiling tiles rattling from bass amps and sneakers sticking to the linoleum floor. Black Flag, Sonic Youth, The Replacements, and Alex Chilton tore through that stage without velvet ropes.

FILE ENTRY #02B // CRITTENDEN COUNTY COURTHOUSE · 2007

The Imagine Piano, DiAnne Price & The Sacred Pilgrimage

Parallel to restoring French antiques and oil paintings at Brice Antiques (1997–2007), a life in high-level touring hospitality emerged. In 2007, I accompanied beloved Memphis blues and jazz treasure DiAnne Price as she performed on John Lennon’s original Steinway upright piano—the very instrument on which Lennon composed “Imagine” at Tittenhurst Park in 1971.

The piano was owned by George Michael, who sent it traveling across America and the world on a pilgrimage to locations that had experienced profound tragedy and devastation. In George Michael’s eyes, the wrongful conviction of the West Memphis Three at the Crittenden County Courthouse qualified as one of the deep tragedies of our time.

The piano was placed on the courthouse lawn in Marion, Arkansas. Leaning over the instrument beside DiAnne, you could still clearly see the cigarette burn marks in the walnut wood right next to the keyboard where Lennon laid down his smoke while working out the chords.

FILE ENTRY #02C // IRVING PLAZA (NYC) TO BLUESVILLE (TUNICA) · 2002–2019

The B-52s: 17 Years of Touring, VIP Sanctuary & Memphis Soul Food

On February 4, 2002 at Irving Plaza in New York City, The B-52s celebrated their landmark 25th anniversary birthday concert. I served as VIP host that evening—igniting an enduring 17-year touring and hospitality relationship with the band that stretched from Manhattan ballrooms to the Mid-South's own Horseshoe Casino Bluesville venue in Tunica.

Whenever the B's rolled through the Mid-South, I would take Kate Pierson and band members "bouncing Memphis for soul food"—riding the vintage Main Street trolley cars down to beloved local holes-in-the-wall like DejaVu Cajun & Creole Soul Food. Pictured outside DejaVu with Kate is Tracy Wormworth, the legendary bassist of 1980s new-wave icons The Waitresses ("I Know What Boys Like"), who has held down the low-end groove for The B-52s for decades. Backstage at Horseshoe Casino's Bluesville venue in Tunica, we were joined by close friend JW Whitten, longtime career advisor and confidant to rock & roll pioneer Jerry Lee Lewis. It was an unspoken partnership across American music lore: I kept us backstage at the pop and rock; JW kept us backstage at Country Western and legendary roots shows. We were always rolling up in either my Cadillac Eldorado or his STS to see our friends play and hang out after the house lights came up. That bond remains unbroken today—the bed I sleep on, the TV I watch, and the washing machine at Spottswood House are all from JW.

That work expanded into more than fifteen years of discreet artist hospitality, touring wardrobe coordination, and artist sanctuaries for major festivals and music legends. The golden rule of VIP care: genuine warmth, zero pretension, complete discretion, and knowing where the real greens, cornbread, and crawfish étouffée are in Memphis.

FILE ENTRY #02D // BACKSTAGE LORE · PARLIAMENT-FUNKADELIC

The P-Funk Mothership Connection: Gary Shieder (“Starchild”)

Beyond The B-52s, decades of high-level music production and backstage life connected me with true American musical royalty. Among the most indelible encounters was with Gary Shieder—the beloved musical director, lead guitarist, and vocalist for George Clinton’s Parliament-Funkadelic, inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997.

Famous worldwide as “Starchild,” Shieder famously wore his iconic trademark white-or-blue diaper and personal “Gary” ballcap on stage every single night, bringing cosmic funk, gospel grit, and searing guitar leads to anthems like “One Nation Under a Groove,” “Cosmic Slop,” and “Atomic Dog.”

Captured backstage in raw, unfiltered glory: Stevan in a red camp shirt, black trousers, and pompadour haircut standing alongside Shieder in his signature diaper. Unvarnished backstage truth that no corporate bio could ever manufacture.

FILE ENTRY #02E // THE BEAUTY SHOP & COOPER-YOUNG

DJ Steve Anne: Monday Night Rehab & The Reverse-Surprise Float

Following Brice Antiques, Halloween 2007 launched the full-time moniker DJ Steve Anne. Anchored by the legendary Monday night "Rehab" retro residency at The Beauty Shop in Cooper-Young—co-DJed with Piper Dandy, daughter of southern rock icon Jim Dandy—I was spinning up to eight gigs a week across Dish (Tea Dance, White Trash Wednesdays), Mollie Fontaine Lounge in Victorian Village, and EP’s on Beale.

Every year, my birthday parties at Monday Rehab transformed the entire Beauty Shop restaurant into an unhinged, packed throw-down with live bands and relentless DJ sets. But one year, I orchestrated the ultimate reverse surprise that no one was ready for: instead of arriving at a party, I pulled up outside 966 S. Cooper St. on a full illuminated parade float trimmed in shimmering gold fringe and red banquet booths—spinning live behind my turntables with my band dancing on the front deck of the float.

Between 250 and 300 stunned partygoers poured into Cooper Street in absolute disbelief. The shock and wild pandemonium of that night is the exact reason only two photographs exist to document the spectacle. Documented by Memphis Magazine (“The Transformers”) and headlining four consecutive years for the Friends for Life Halloween benefits at Bridges ("Shipwrecked" and "Under the Big Top"), the DJ decks were about sheer room tempo, physical spectacle, and high-contrast typography flyers that continue to define the TallSlimCollins aesthetic today.

2010s – 2020s Conservation, Fine Art & Studio Practice
FILE ENTRY #03A // BAR DKDC & THE ARCADE

Bar DKDC, 10 Years of Live Music & Daisy Glaze Film Production

For nearly a decade, I ran Bar DKDC and The Beauty Shop—booking live music six nights a week, operating sound and lights, solo bartending 100-capacity crowds open to close, and earning a 2016 Thrillist feature for "Best Bartenders in Memphis."

That hospitality pedigree was forged directly across the street at Maxwell’s (corner of Cooper & Young), sitting across the bar from Memphis's two finest bartenders ever: David Parks (“The Sheriff of the Twilight Zone”) and Sallie Annaser (“The Monster of Martinis”). That was where I met my brother Shea and learned how to command a room, read a crowd, and pour with precision—mentors I proudly cited in that Thrillist profile.

In 2022, my spatial eye and grip skills carried into cinema: serving as Production Designer for the Daisy Glaze music video "Ghost of Elvis Presley" (directed by Michele Civetta, DP Robert Opperman, produced by Sonic Boom). Transforming historic Memphis landmarks like The Arcade Restaurant and Molly Fontaine Lounge, we captured the haunting cinematic essence of the city on 35mm film.

FILE ENTRY #03B // MADISON & DANNY THOMAS

The Rubber Stamp Basement Studio & Decadence Manor Salvage

While downtown Memphis was renovating the historic Advance Rubber Stamp Building on Madison & Danny Thomas, I established my creative atelier in its deep brick basement. In that subterranean workshop, surrounded by historic printing presses, I began painting large-scale botanical textile wall art and experimenting with waterfast inks on raw canvas.

Up the street, Decadence Manor stood as Midtown’s monument to Southern bohemian freedom: grandfather clock faces leaning against weathered porch railings, motorcycle frames resting among magnolia blossoms, and artists lounging on velvet daybeds until sunrise. Rescuing architectural salvage before developer bulldozers arrived became a core preservation mission.

Present Day Spottswood House & The Dual Practice
FILE ENTRY #04A // SPOTTSWOOD CORRIDOR, 901

The Dual Practice: Home Revival & TallSlimCollins

Today, operating out of Spottswood House in Memphis, the work is channeled through two complementary disciplines that honor the firewall between service and storytelling:

Home Revival by Stevan Collins Lazich (stevancollinslazich.com): A high-touch residential transformation service specializing in architectural color palette translation, drywall repair, fine finishing, custom lighting, and room flow revivals. Grounded, practical, and 100% clean of moniker or tattoo lore (*"Revive. Don't Replace."*).

TallSlimCollins (tallslimcollins.com): The creative umbrella, storytelling archive, traditional trade flash series, and wearable design atelier. Here, every garment, print, and dispatch is an authentic artifact from four decades of living Mid-South lore.

FILE ENTRY #04B // THE ATELIER LAB

The Honest-Work Mythology: Shop Dogs, Swallows & Artifacts

Merchandise in the TallSlimCollins world never exists in a vacuum. A shirt isn't a logo slapped on cotton—it's a physical relic from a real night, a lost room, or an oral tradition.

From the Shop Dog Society honoring the faithful companions snoozing in workbench sawdust, to the Spottswood Swallows carrying tape measures and extension cords, to upcoming NAVETS capsule releases: each piece is an invitation to step into a complete cultural world.
