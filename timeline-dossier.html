1980s – 1990s Drapery Workrooms, Scale & French Antiquities
FILE ENTRY #01A // 583 S. MCLEAN & 1707 MADISON

The Antique Shop Sink & The Architecture of Scale

In 1984, in the fourth grade, my family moved to Memphis to renovate 583 S. McLean. By 1985, home was literally the interior of an antique store at 1707 Madison Avenue. Education didn't happen in air-conditioned classrooms—it happened on workroom cutting tables, surrounded by heavy bolts of French silk damask, cast brass fittings, and aged walnut patina.

When you're a fifth-grader taking nightly baths in a stainless commercial kitchen sink between 18th-century French armoires, you develop an intimate, unromantic relationship with physical objects. You learn what most modern designers never touch: weight, structural proportion, and honesty. If a timber frame wasn't plumb, you felt it. If drapery fabric wasn't weighted, it fell lifeless.

Midtown streetlife was raw and unscripted. Mowing the corner park across from the shop for $35 to earn a Libertyland season pass meant pulling empty Stag bottles from azalea shrubs and hosing down neighborhood legends like Dancing Jimmy under radiating streetlights.

FILE ENTRY #01B // COOPER-YOUNG & SUN STUDIO

Cottland Bedding, Otherlands Buildout & Sun Studio (1992–1997)

In 1992, I began at Cottland Bedding Company, learning retail craft, futons, and exotic gifts. In 1994, we stripped plaster, framed partitions, and renovated the adjoining space to open Otherlands Coffee Bar in Cooper-Young. It became an institution: precision barista standards, sensory cleanliness, and gift buying.

During those Midtown years, I worked the front door at Automatic Slim’s for Karen Carrier—checking IDs for legends like Link Wray—and spent the sweltering summer of 1997 working seasonal hours at Sun Studio, right when Parliament-Funkadelic tore through the University of Memphis. It cemented the intersection of historic preservation, live performance, and physical space.

1990s – 2010s Subculture, VIP Hosting & Midnight Vinyl
FILE ENTRY #02A // 1783 UNION REAR & 1588 MADISON

Babylon Café & The Antenna Club: Midnight In Midnight Memphis

Between 1991 and 1993, I operated as the all-around "Boy Friday" at Babylon Café, tucked into the gravel alleyway behind 1783 Union Rear. Hand-rolling pasta on long wooden boards, simmering scratch marinara, and grinding espresso in the Herb Room while Cyndi Lauper, Laurie Anderson, or delegations of Tibetan monks in saffron robes slipped in through the back door at midnight.

Down the street at 1588 Madison Avenue, The Antenna Club was where underground Memphis kept its teeth. Narrow as a bowling alley, with tin ceiling tiles rattling from bass amps and sneakers sticking to the linoleum floor. Black Flag, Sonic Youth, The Replacements, and Alex Chilton tore through that stage without velvet ropes.

FILE ENTRY #02B // IRVING PLAZA & COURTHOUSE STEPS

The Imagine Piano, The B-52s & Touring Hospitality

Parallel to restoring French antiques and oil paintings at Brice Antiques (1997–2007), a life in high-level touring hospitality emerged. In 2007, I stood on the Crittenden County courthouse steps in West Memphis—where the West Memphis Three were wrongfully convicted—accompanying Dianne Price performing on John Lennon’s original Imagine piano. Owned by George Michael and organized by his assistant Caroline True, the piano traveled across America to tragedy locations, heading next to Virginia Tech.

Beginning February 4, 2002 at Irving Plaza in NYC (their 25th anniversary), I began a 17-year touring and VIP hosting relationship with The B-52s, from Manhattan to Tunica’s Bluesville. That work expanded into more than fifteen years of discreet artist hospitality, touring wardrobe coordination, and green room production for major artists, festivals, and music legends.

FILE ENTRY #02C // THE BEAUTY SHOP & COOPER-YOUNG

DJ Steve Anne: Monday Night Rehab & The Decks

Following Brice Antiques, Halloween 2007 launched the full-time moniker DJ Steve Anne. Anchored by the legendary Monday night "Rehab" retro residency at The Beauty Shop (co-DJed with Piper Dandy, daughter of Jim Dandy), I was spinning up to eight gigs a week across Dish (Tea Dance, White Trash Wednesdays), Mollie Fontaine Lounge, and EP’s on Beale.

Documented by Memphis Magazine (“The Transformers”) and spinning four consecutive years for the Friends for Life Halloween benefits at Bridges ("Shipwrecked" and "Under the Big Top"), the DJ decks were about timing, room tempo, and high-contrast typography flyers that still inspire every Threadless drop today.

2010s – 2020s Conservation, Fine Art & Studio Practice
FILE ENTRY #03A // BAR DKDC & THE ARCADE

Bar DKDC, 10 Years of Live Music & Daisy Glaze Film Production

For nearly a decade, I ran Bar DKDC and The Beauty Shop—booking live music six nights a week, operating sound and lights, solo bartending 100-capacity crowds open to close, and earning a 2016 Thrillist feature for "Best Bartenders in Memphis."

In 2022, my spatial eye and grip skills carried into cinema: serving as Production Designer for the Daisy Glaze music video "Ghost of Elvis Presley" (directed by Michele Civetta, DP Robert Opperman, produced by Sonic Boom). Transforming historic Memphis landmarks like The Arcade Restaurant and Molly Fontaine Lounge, we captured the haunting cinematic essence of the city on 35mm film.

FILE ENTRY #03B // MADISON & DANNY THOMAS

The Rubber Stamp Basement Studio & Decadence Manor Salvage

While downtown Memphis was renovating the historic Advance Rubber Stamp Building on Madison & Danny Thomas, I established my creative atelier in its deep brick basement. In that subterranean workshop, surrounded by historic printing presses, I began painting large-scale botanical textile wall art and experimenting with waterfast inks on raw canvas.

Up the street, Decadence Manor stood as Midtown’s monument to Southern bohemian freedom: grandfather clock faces leaning against weathered porch railings, motorcycle frames resting among magnolia blossoms, and artists lounging on velvet daybeds until sunrise. Rescuing architectural salvage before developer bulldozers arrived became a core preservation mission.

Present Day Spottswood House & The Dual Practice
FILE ENTRY #04A // SPOTTSWOOD CORRIDOR, 901

The Dual Practice: Home Revival & TallSlimCollins

Today, operating out of Spottswood House in Memphis, the work is channeled through two complementary disciplines that honor the firewall between service and storytelling:

Home Revival by Stevan Collins Lazich (stevancollinslazich.com): A high-touch residential transformation service specializing in architectural color palette translation, drywall repair, fine finishing, custom lighting, and room flow revivals. Grounded, practical, and 100% clean of moniker or tattoo lore (*"Revive. Don't Replace."*).

TallSlimCollins (tallslimcollins.com): The creative umbrella, storytelling archive, traditional trade flash series, and wearable design atelier. Here, every garment, print, and dispatch is an authentic artifact from four decades of living Mid-South lore.

FILE ENTRY #04B // THE ATELIER LAB

The Honest-Work Mythology: Shop Dogs, Swallows & Artifacts

Merchandise in the TallSlimCollins world never exists in a vacuum. A shirt isn't a logo slapped on cotton—it's a physical relic from a real night, a lost room, or an oral tradition.

From the Shop Dog Society honoring the faithful companions snoozing in workbench sawdust, to the Spottswood Swallows carrying tape measures and extension cords, to upcoming NAVETS capsule releases: each piece is an invitation to step into a complete cultural world.
